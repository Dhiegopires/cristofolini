Good design earns its place in the roadmap.

Most design teams optimize for aesthetics. I optimize for decisions. Every screen is a hypothesis about user behavior, and the work only matters if the product performs better because of it.

Where design goes wrong

Products fail when design is treated like decoration.

Teams design for demos, not for retention. They ship polished onboarding screens and skip the moment users realize they do not know what to do next. A decade of work across SaaS, Fintech, and B2B has been about fixing that gap between what a product looks like and what it actually does for the business.

That means starting with friction, conversion, activation, comprehension, and scale. Visual quality matters, but only after the product becomes easier to understand, easier to use, and easier to grow.

My approach

01

Start with the metric, not the mockup.

The first question is never what the screen should look like. It is what needs to improve: activation, conversion, clarity, speed, trust, or retention.

02

Design systems beat isolated screens.

Reusable structure is what keeps teams fast and products coherent. Components, tokens, and interaction rules matter because scale eventually exposes every shortcut.

03

Specs should help engineering move faster.

Good design work reduces ambiguity. Clear flows, naming, edge cases, and interaction states save time for the whole team and make shipping less fragile.
