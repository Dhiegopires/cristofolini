Color Palette

Decision: warm dark background, single amber accent, no secondary accent. Justification below references the explicit anti-reference from Step 1 discovery and the verified competitor set from Step 1.3 market research.

Background theme: warm dark. The explicit anti-reference given in discovery was "qualquer light mode como apple.com." All four independently verified competitor portfolios in the market research (Stephen Calvillo, Josh Mateo, Marcos Rezende, Francesco Fagioli) use light or neutral themes. Warm dark is not a safe default here, it is the one direction none of the four verified comparisons take.

One accent, used for every CTA, focus ring, link, and highlight on the site. Warm amber reads as confident and crafted rather than corporate. It is also distinct from the neutral, near-colorless palettes of all four verified competitor portfolios, none of which use a saturated warm accent as their signature color.

Secondary accent: none. The candidate's work spans SaaS and Fintech, but these are not two audiences requiring two visual tones, they are one audience (recruiters and hiring managers) evaluating one thing (judgment and craft). A second accent would dilute the single signature color rather than serve a real segmentation need.

Typography

Visual Mood

Three words, then the metaphor that should resolve any future design decision not explicitly covered elsewhere.

Blocked

Hard edges, hairline borders, zero border-radius on every interactive element. Nothing rounds off a decision.

Bold

Display type at display size. Headlines are never apologetic or small.

Technical

Mono type for data, real numbers in every hero, token-style notation where it's literally true (the design system case).

Infrastructure, not decoration.

This is the candidate's own language, taken directly from the Fiter case study headline ("Infrastructure first. Design second.") and from the Vellumwire Design System case's entire premise. The site should look load-bearing: square edges, visible structure, hairline grids you can see the joints of. Nothing on the page should look ornamental. If a design decision doesn't serve legibility or hierarchy, it doesn't belong, the same standard applied to the actual product work being shown.

04

What We Are Not

Specific patterns ruled out, each with a reason tied to discovery answers or research findings, not a vague style preference.

No

Light mode in any form. Named explicitly as the anti-reference in discovery ("qualquer light mode como apple.com").

No

Rounded corners on buttons, inputs, or cards. Every interactive surface in the shipped CSS uses border-radius: 0. A rounded button would be the single most visible inconsistency on the site.

No

Stock-photo hero imagery or generic SaaS dashboard mockups. Every hero image on the site is either a real product screen or a real portrait, never a placeholder.

No

AI-pattern copy: em dashes, "not X but Y" contrast framing, unnamed "most teams" claims. Named directly in discovery ("já fizemos infinitas auditorias sobre isso") and enforced across every case study this session.

No

Generic role jargon: "user-centered," "innovative," "passionate." Flagged explicitly in discovery as language to avoid.

Approval

  • Color palette: warm dark, single amber accent
  • Typography: Bebas Neue, Syne, IBM Plex Mono
  • Visual mood: blocked, bold, technical, "infrastructure not decoration"

Since this identity is already live in production, approval here means: lock it. Any future change to color, type, or the zero-radius rule is a deliberate scope decision, not a drive-by tweak during a content or copy session.
