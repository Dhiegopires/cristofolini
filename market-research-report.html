Market Overview

Findings from live web research conducted today. Anything not independently confirmed is marked ESTIMATE.

The market is stabilizing, not booming, and it favors senior over junior

"Senior practitioners and generalist roles are recovering faster than entry-level positions, which remain scarce and highly competitive." The broader correction took roughly two decades to produce today's oversupply at the junior level, and is not expected to reverse quickly. VERIFIED

Tech layoffs tracked through layoffs.fyi show design and product roles cut at rates closer to engineering than to recruiting or HR, which bore the brunt of headcount reductions overall. No SaaS- or fintech-specific, or remote-specific, quantitative breakdown could be found. PARTIAL: sector-specific demand is anecdotal, not measured

Sources: Nielsen Norman Group, "State of UX 2026" · Carlo Ciccarelli, "How Saturated Is the UX Job Market?" · layoffs.fyi · UXPA/User Interviews 2026 Salary Report

Recruiters screen against three things, not a vibe check

The most credible, named source on this is Julie Zhuo (Director of Product Design, Facebook), describing the screening checklist used there: the idea (is there a real rationale behind what was built), usability, and craftsmanship (did the designer sweat the details). Candidates were asked to critique existing products live, not just present their own work.

Source: Julie Zhuo, via First Round Review, "An Inside Look at Facebook's Method for Hiring Designers"

A screening layer now runs before any human opens the site

One source claims 78% of recruiters now use AI-assisted tools to pre-filter portfolios, and that a candidate's name plus role keyword must appear in the page title and meta description to surface at all. The 78% figure has no disclosed methodology in the source itself. UNVERIFIED STATISTIC, directionally plausible

This directly matches a finding from this project's own Site Audit (step 1.2): the home page title and meta description already carry "Senior Product Designer," but the visible H1 does not.

Source: Muzli Blog, "How to Build a UX Portfolio That Actually Gets You Hired (2026)"

The single most-cited rejection reason is unclear individual ownership

Zhuo's "No-Hire" series names lack of intentionality as reason one. Separately, multiple independent hiring-guide sources converge on the same senior-specific failure: "candidates were unclear which work was done by them versus other designers on their team," and "senior candidates aren't able to speak to the business impact... of their work."

This is the exact gap this project corrected today, before this research was run: two case studies (Vellumwire, Vellumwire Design System) were rewritten this session specifically because authorship was ambiguous, either defaulting to "we" on solo work or using no first-person language at all.

Sources: Julie Zhuo, "7 Reasons to No-Hire a Product Designer" (Parts I-II) · Underdog.io, "Product Design Portfolio Examples"

Opportunity Map

Where cristofolini.site already matches what the research says works, where it's ahead, and where the gap actually is.

Individual ownership is now explicit, which is the single most-cited rejection reason elsewhere

Zhuo's "lack of intentionality" and the recurring "unclear individual contribution" complaint are the two most consistently named reasons senior candidates get passed over. This project corrected exactly that today, rewriting two case studies that previously used "we" on solo work or no first-person language at all. None of the four verified competitor portfolios were checked for this specific failure mode, but the research independently confirms it's the right thing to have fixed.

A dark, bold, blocked identity is a real point of difference

All four verified competitors use light or neutral, conventionally "clean" presentation. None use a dark, bold, blocked visual language as their core identity. This matches the explicitly stated preference for this project (blocked, minimalist, bold, not light mode) and is not a compromise: it's a differentiator against the verified set, provided execution quality stays high enough to read as intentional rather than unfinished, which is the self-identified gap from the Site Audit.

Impact-first framing already matches what the research says senior portfolios get wrong

"Most senior designers are still building portfolios like they're applying for IC roles, showcasing execution when they should be demonstrating impact." Every case study on this site leads its hero with a numbers-first impact grid before any screen is shown. This is already aligned with the research, not a gap.

NDA handling precedent exists and matches a real competitor pattern

Two of four verified competitors (Rezende, Fagioli) handle confidential work with an explicit disclosure or access-gate rather than silently omitting it. This site already discloses NDA status in case-study metadata (MedMe). The pattern is validated; nothing further required beyond consistency.
