Veriflow

Pricing and plans concept for a SaaS product

UI/UX Design · 2024 · Concept project

Overview

Veriflow is a pricing page concept for a B2B SaaS product. Pricing pages that lead with feature lists put the user in comparison mode. The design problem is earlier: what is each plan actually for, and who should pick it?

The focus was on hierarchy and copy that leads with the answer.

Plan structure

One tier is highlighted as recommended, but not aggressively. The labels describe who each plan is for rather than what it includes, so users figure out where they fit without having to compare everything line by line.

The grid is designed for someone arriving uncertain. The layout does most of the work before they even read the features.

Making the cost visible

Below the plans, the page shifts from "here's what you get" to "here's what staying put costs". The numbers are specific: compliance risk, hours lost, unseen overhead.

Concrete figures do more to move someone than a feature list. The goal was to make inaction feel like the expensive choice.

Social proof

The testimonials sit below the pricing, not above it. By that point the user has already looked at the plans, so the quotes are there to confirm rather than pitch. Each one names a specific result, not a general feeling.

Closing

The FAQ covers the objections that actually stop people from signing up, not a generic Q&A. The footer stays clean: legal links, secondary CTA, nothing extra.
